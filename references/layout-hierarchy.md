# Layout and Hierarchy

## Principle

A user should know what to read, what to look at, and what to do next without scanning the entire screen.

## Preferred hierarchy pattern

A strong screen usually has:
1. one primary message or task;
2. one supporting explanation;
3. one primary visual or data region;
4. one primary action;
5. quieter metadata/secondary actions.

Avoid several headings, cards, badges, banners, and CTAs competing at the same visual weight.

## Editorial/product storytelling

When presenting work:
- use a clear section title;
- give each product a strong name;
- keep explanation visibly smaller;
- use real product screenshots as proof;
- keep metadata tiny but legible;
- separate projects with rhythm, lines, or whitespace instead of card clutter.

Milestones, dates, categories, and status should read as metadata, not compete with the product name.

## Landing pages

Lead with the benefit or product truth.

Good:
- "See who showed up."
- "Get paid to leave."
- concrete outcome/product language.

Weak:
- generic category claims;
- adjectives without proof;
- stacked paragraphs before the user knows what the product is.

A hero should not become a poster for empty words.

## Dashboards

Use actual product terminology.

Do not rename metrics for aesthetics if the product already has canonical names.

Prioritize:
- current state;
- meaningful deltas;
- user identity/context when relevant;
- relationships between data;
- next useful action.

Avoid:
- fake "overview" cards;
- duplicated summaries;
- charts without interpretation;
- decorative agent/AI status if it is not real.

## Cards and collections

Cards should exist because items are independently actionable or comparable—not because every UI needs cards.

Rules:
- do not copy another product's card structure exactly;
- do not let controls cover content;
- background/inactive cards should not steal interaction;
- keep action placement stable;
- clamp or handle long content cleanly;
- use fewer columns when larger content improves comprehension.

## Navigation

Prefer a simple primary navigation.

Do not duplicate the same destination in:
- top nav;
- utility nav;
- quick links;
- extra card menus

unless there is a clear reason.

On mobile, hiding chrome behind one clear control can be better than shrinking a full desktop navigation.

## Composition

Use alignment and grids intentionally.

Good composition can be:
- centered and calm;
- asymmetric/editorial;
- split-screen;
- rail + content;
- full-bleed media.

Choose based on the product.

Do not force a centered SaaS hero onto every page.
